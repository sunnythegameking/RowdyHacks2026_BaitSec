Text talking about ways people get you with a unexpected opportunities and how to prevent it

Unexpected opportunity scams usually start with an unexpected text, email, or social media DM offering an exclusive financial windfall. They say you have “won a major sweepstakes,” “qualified for a government grant,” or been selected for an “exclusive investment loop” that guarantees massive returns. Once you engage, they’ll show you professional-looking documents, fake certificates, or digital balances showing your supposed wealth. (They’re fake.) At some point, the organizer will ask you to pay an upfront fee — usually for "taxes," "processing," or "customs clearance" — before they can release your money. But if you pay that fee, not only is your money gone, you’ll never see a dime of that promised fortune.

Key Signs

  • You Didn't Enter: You receive an announcement for a lottery, contest, or draw that you have no memory of entering or participating in.
  • Guaranteed Returns: They offer an investment, crypto pool, or betting system and promise 100% guaranteed profits with "zero risk."
  • Secrecy Requests: The person tells you to keep the opportunity a secret from your family, friends, or bank tellers so they don't "steal your spot."

Ways to prevent scams

  • Delete and Block: Do not reply to unexpected messages offering money, grants, or investments. Replying lets scammers know your number is active.
  • Verify Through Official Channels: If a message claims to be from a real agency (like the government or a major brand), look up their official contact info independently. Never use the links or phone numbers provided in the message.
  • Keep Your Money in Your Pocket: Never pay upfront fees, taxes, or processing charges to claim a prize or start an investment. Legitimate windfalls always deduct fees from the payout.
