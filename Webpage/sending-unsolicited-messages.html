Text talking about ways people get you with unsolicited messages and how to prevent it

Getting unsolicited messages usually starts with an unexpected text, WhatsApp chat, or email from an unknown number or someone pretending to have dialed the wrong person. They say something casual like “Is this [Name]?” or offer an accidental greeting to spark a conversation. Once you reply, they will quickly shift to acting overly friendly, sharing attractive photos, or introducing a "business partner" who makes easy money. (They’re fake.) At some point, the stranger will try to move the conversation to an unmonitored app or convince you to visit a specific platform to invest or buy a service. But if you keep engaging, not only will they exploit your trust, you’ll eventually be funneled into a financial or identity scam.

  • Ignore generic and unexpected greetings from numbers you do not recognize. Real people trying to reach a friend will realize their mistake and stop messaging rather than trying to build a new relationship.
  • Never reply to opt-out of text spam (like texting "STOP") unless it is a verified, short-code marketing message from a brand you actually use. Replying to a scammer just proves your number is active.
  • Don’t trust anyone who moves you to a different app like Telegram, WhatsApp, or Line right after making contact. Honest professionals and legitimate businesses do not rely on random personal chat accounts to find clients.

Key Signs

  • The "Wrong Number" Hook: The sender pretends they meant to text someone else but uses it as an excuse to keep chatting with you anyway.
  • Suspicious Link Shorteners: The message includes cropped or unusual links (like bit.ly, tinyurl, or completely random letter combinations) to hide the real destination.
  • Vague Job or Cash Offers: It promises high pay or free items for minimal effort without explaining what the actual company is.

Ways to prevent scams

  • Use Your Phone's Built-in Filters: Enable "Filter Unknown Senders" on iOS or "Spam Protection" on Android to automatically catch these messages before they hit your main inbox.
  • Report and Block Instantly: Use your app's built-in tool to report the message as spam. On mobile networks, you can also forward spam texts directly to 7726 (SPAM) to alert your carrier.
  • Hide Your Contact Details: Avoid posting your phone number or primary email address publicly on social media, marketplace listings, or unverified online forums where scrapers can steal them.
