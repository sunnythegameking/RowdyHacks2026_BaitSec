Text talking about ways people get you with asking for your personal info and how to prevent it

Personal information scams usually start with an unexpected text, email, or call claiming there is an urgent problem with one of your accounts. They say you must "verify your identity" or "confirm your details" immediately to unlock your account or avoid a fine. Once you click their link, you'll see a professional-looking website or form that asks for your Social Security number, password, or banking codes. (They're fake.) At some point, the platform will use those details to lock you out of your real accounts or open fraudulent credit lines in your name. But if you fill out the form, not only is your private data compromised, you'll never regain control of the security breach.

  • Ignore generic and unexpected alerts about account suspensions, tax issues, or package deliveries. Real companies will never ask you to resolve security issues through text links.
  • Never share one-time passcodes or PINs with anyone, even if they claim to be a customer service representative. That's a sure sign of a scam.
  • Don't trust anyone who asks for government IDs or financial details over social media or unverified chat apps. No honest organization operates that way.

Key Signs

  • Mismatched Links or Emails: The sender's email address looks slightly off (e.g., support@paypa1-security.com instead of paypal.com), or the website URL doesn't match the actual company name.
  • Generic Greetings: The message addresses you as "Dear Customer" or "Valued Member" rather than using your actual name, because scammers blast these messages to thousands of people at once.
  • Requests for Security Data: They explicitly ask for things a real company will never ask you to reveal, such as passwords, PINs, or one-time verification codes sent to your phone.

Ways to prevent scams

  • Put your phone on the Do Not Call list DO NOT CALL REGISTRY
  • Never send personal details over the phone or in an email if someone ask for it
  • Check Your Credit every now and then to make sure no one has opened anything on your credit
